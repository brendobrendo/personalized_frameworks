# Evolution of Computing and Payment Networks

A chronological timeline tracing the evolution of modern computing alongside the development of the **Visa**, **ACH**, and **SWIFT** payment networks.

|          **9/18/1958** | **BankAmericard launches** — Bank of America launches BankAmericard in Fresno, California, establishing the credit-card program that eventually evolves into Visa and creating a growing need for automated transaction processing.                                                                                                                                                            |
| **1/1/1959 (approx.)** | **ERMA enters banking operations** — Bank of America begins deploying its Electronic Recording Machine, Accounting (ERMA) system, using computers and magnetic-ink character recognition to automate check processing and account bookkeeping.                                                                                                                                                 |
|          **7/30/1959** | **Noyce files integrated-circuit patent** — Robert Noyce files for a practical monolithic integrated circuit using Fairchild Semiconductor's planar process, helping make mass production of silicon integrated circuits possible.                                                                                                                                                             |
|           **4/7/1964** | **IBM announces System/360** — IBM introduces a compatible family of mainframe computers, allowing businesses and banks to expand computing capacity without replacing their entire software environment and helping establish mainframes as the computational backbone of large financial institutions.                                                                                       |
| **1/1/1965 (approx.)** | **Minicomputers begin spreading** — Machines such as DEC's PDP-8 dramatically reduce the cost and size of computers, expanding computing beyond centralized corporate and government mainframes.                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| **1/1/1968 (approx.)** | **Banks begin developing ACH concept** — California bankers form the Special Committee on Paperless Entries (SCOPE) in response to rapidly increasing check volumes, beginning an institutional effort to replace some paper checks with electronic payment instructions.                                                                                                                      |
|         **10/29/1969** | **First ARPANET host-to-host connection** — UCLA and Stanford Research Institute establish an early packet-switched computer connection, helping demonstrate the networking technology that eventually develops into the Internet.                                                                                                                                                             |
|         **11/15/1971** | **Intel introduces the 4004 microprocessor** — Intel publicly introduces a general-purpose CPU contained on a single chip, opening the path toward inexpensive personal computers, terminals, ATMs, and embedded computerized devices.                                                                                                                                                         |

| **1/1/1975 (approx.)** | **Electronic card networks expand into debit** — BankAmericard's infrastructure begins supporting debit transactions, extending computerized card processing from revolving credit toward payments drawn directly from deposit accounts.                                                                                                                                                       |
| **1/1/1976 (approx.)** | **BankAmericard becomes Visa** — BankAmericard is renamed Visa, providing the increasingly international payment network with a common global identity.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
|           **5/9/1977** | **SWIFT network goes live** — SWIFT begins operating its international electronic messaging network with 518 institutions in 22 countries, allowing standardized financial messages to move between banks electronically rather than primarily through telex.                                                                                                                                  |
| **6/1/1977 (approx.)** | **Apple II reaches consumers** — The Apple II becomes one of the first successful mass-market personal computers, helping move computing from specialized institutions into homes, schools, and small businesses.                                                                                                                                                                              |
|          **8/12/1981** | **IBM introduces the PC** — IBM launches the IBM Personal Computer, legitimizing microprocessor-based computing for corporate use and helping establish the architecture that dominates the PC industry.                                                                                                                                                                                       |
|           **1/1/1983** | **ARPANET transitions to TCP/IP** — ARPANET adopts TCP/IP, establishing a common protocol through which independent computer networks can communicate and providing the fundamental architecture of the modern Internet.                                                                                                                                                                       |
| **1/1/1983 (approx.)** | **Visa expands global ATM connectivity** — Visa develops international ATM-network capabilities, connecting computerized banking terminals across institutions and extending electronic financial networks directly to consumers.                                                                                                                                                              |
|          **1/24/1984** | **Apple introduces Macintosh** — Apple launches the Macintosh, helping popularize graphical user interfaces and the mouse and making computers substantially easier for nontechnical users to operate.                                                                                                                                                                                         |

|          **6/29/2007** | **Apple releases the iPhone** — The iPhone combines a general-purpose computer, Internet connection, touchscreen, sensors, and eventually secure payment credentials in a device consumers carry continuously, establishing a major platform for mobile banking and payments.                                                                                                                  |

|         **11/30/2022** | **ChatGPT launches** — OpenAI releases ChatGPT publicly, bringing large language models to a mass consumer audience and accelerating the transition toward computers capable of interpreting and generating natural language.                                                                                                                                                                  |

## The Larger Pattern

### 1958–1971: Computers Become Economically Scalable

Integrated circuits, compatible mainframes, and eventually microprocessors dramatically reduce the cost of computation. Banks become some of the earliest large-scale commercial users because accounting, check processing, transaction records, and payment authorization are unusually well suited to automation.

### 1969–1995: Computers Become Networked

Computer networking develops alongside specialized financial networks.

* **ACH** connects U.S. bank accounts.
* **SWIFT** standardizes messages exchanged between financial institutions.
* **Visa** connects merchants, acquiring banks, issuing banks, and cardholders.
* **TCP/IP and the Internet** provide a general-purpose architecture for connecting computer networks.

These systems share the same fundamental idea: standardized protocols allow computers belonging to different organizations to exchange information automatically.

### 1995–2010: Networked Computing Reaches the Consumer

The commercial Internet, encryption, PCs, broadband, cloud computing, and smartphones increasingly allow consumers to interact directly with financial computer systems.

Activities that once required a bank employee, paper instrument, telephone call, or specialized terminal increasingly become software interactions.

### 2010–Present: Computing Becomes Embedded in the Payment System

Smartphones, cloud computing, APIs, cryptography, tokenization, biometrics, machine learning, and AI increasingly blur the distinction between a **computer network** and a **payment network**.

**Visa** becomes a specialized global computer network for authorizing, clearing, and settling card transactions.

**SWIFT** becomes a specialized secure network for transmitting standardized financial messages between institutions.

**ACH** becomes standardized electronic infrastructure for transmitting instructions that debit and credit bank accounts.

The evolution can therefore be summarized as:

> **Cheaper computation → networked computers → standardized financial networks → Internet connectivity → cloud computing → smartphones → real-time digital payments → AI-driven computing infrastructure**
